Johnny Depp's Ebenezer Tops the Box Office — His First No. 1 Opening in Eight Years

Hollywood
Nov 17, 2026
3 min read

Johnny Depp's Ebenezer topped the box office with $19M — his first No. 1 opening in eight years. Weekend numbers and what is next.

Advertisement
Advertisement
Advertisement

Johnny Depp is back at the top of the box office. Ebenezer, Ti West's gothic reimagining of Charles Dickens' A Christmas Carol, opened at No. 1 in North America with an estimated $19 million over the November 13–15 weekend, according to studio estimates reported on Sunday, November 15. It is Depp's first No. 1 opening in eight years, since 2018's Fantastic Beasts: The Crimes of Grindelwald.

Advertisement

The film opened in 3,535 theaters through Paramount Pictures and added $23 million from 63 international markets, for a $42 million global debut — already more than its reported $38 million production budget, before marketing costs.

The Comeback, in Numbers

The headline is the comeback, but the numbers around it tell a more careful story. The $19 million opening is well below the $62.2 million that Grindelwald opened with in 2018, and it landed just under Paramount's own $20 million projection. The overall North American weekend was soft, at roughly $86–90 million in total ticket sales.

Advertisement

What the film does have is an audience that came for its star. Exit polling cited by the Associated Press found that about half of moviegoers said they attended because of Depp. Critics were split — the film sits at 52–53 percent on Rotten Tomatoes — but audiences handed it an A- CinemaScore and 4.5 out of 5 on PostTrak.

The Rest of the Weekend

The Cat in the Hat held second place with $11.4 million, and Godzilla Minus Zero took third with $8.3 million in its second weekend, a steep 73 percent drop. Amazon MGM's How to Rob a Bank opened in seventh with $5.1 million on a reported budget above $40 million.

Advertisement

Ramayana: Rise of a Legend added $5.5 million in North America, taking its domestic total to near $24 million.

Advertisement

What Comes Next

The second weekend will matter more than the opening. A $19 million start against a $38 million budget is a solid foundation, not a verdict — and the film's real test is whether the audience that came for Depp brings anyone else with them. The next number that matters arrives on November 20, when The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping opens, with tracking pointing to an $80–100 million weekend that should lift the whole market.

For Depp, though, the symbolism is already settled. Six years after he was asked to leave the Fantastic Beasts franchise, and three years after he said he had "no further need for Hollywood," he has a No. 1 film again — and his next scheduled film, Day Drinker, is due in March.
