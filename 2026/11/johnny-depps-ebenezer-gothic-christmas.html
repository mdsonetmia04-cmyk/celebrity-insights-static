Johnny Depp's Ebenezer: A Gothic Christmas Carol That Has Critics Split Down the Middle

Hollywood
Nov 15, 2026
3 min read

Johnny Depp's Ebenezer is a gothic Christmas Carol from Ti West. Why critics are split on Depp's return to a major studio lead role.

Johnny Depp is back in a major studio lead role with Ebenezer, a gothic reimagining of Charles Dickens' A Christmas Carol that opened in theaters in the United States and the United Kingdom on Friday, November 13, 2026, through Paramount Pictures. Directed by Ti West — the filmmaker behind the X horror trilogy — from a screenplay by Nathaniel Halpern, the film casts Depp as the miserly businessman Ebenezer Scrooge, whose encounters with the spirits of Christmas Past, Present, and Future give him one final chance at redemption.

It is Depp's first major studio release in eight years, and the film's reception is being watched across Hollywood as a test of whether one of the biggest movie stars of his generation can still open a film.

The First Reactions: Warm at the Premiere, Divided in Reviews

The film premiered in New York on November 1, with a global premiere in London's Leicester Square on November 9. Early social-media reactions from those screenings were largely positive, with several critics calling it a wickedly fun holiday treat — and Depp's performance drew praise as energetic and magnetic, with some placing his Scrooge alongside the great screen interpretations of the role.

The full reviews, which arrived after the embargo lifted on November 12, tell a different story. Ebenezer debuted on Rotten Tomatoes at around 50 percent — 51 percent from 59 reviews at first count, hovering near 50 percent as more arrived — with a Metacritic score of 47 to 48 out of 100. At one count, 35 of 69 reviews were fresh and 34 rotten: about as even a split as a film gets. These scores move as reviews are added, so treat them as a snapshot of the opening days, not a final verdict.

The split is as much about Depp as about the film. The Hollywood Reporter said Depp needed a comeback vehicle and found an effective one; others were cooler, calling the film spookily atmospheric but redundant, or fine but forgettable. Audiences, so far, are warmer than critics: early reports put the Rotten Tomatoes audience score at 90 percent, with four stars on PostTrak.

The Cast and the Film

Alongside Depp, the cast includes Ian McKellen as Jacob Marley, Rupert Grint as Bob Cratchit, Tramell Tillman as the Ghost of Christmas Present, Andrea Riseborough as the Ghost of Christmas Past, Sam Claflin as Fred, Daisy Ridley as Emily Cratchit, and Arthur Conti as the young Ebenezer. The film runs 116 minutes and is rated PG-13 in the United States.

West has said he wanted to make the story a more subjective journey, putting viewers inside Scrooge's perspective — a gothic, supernatural take on a story most audiences already know by heart. Paramount is selling it with the tagline "The name you know, the story you don't," and the Friday the 13th release date was no accident: a dark, ghostly take on Dickens arriving on that date is exactly the kind of timing a studio hopes will turn a Christmas film into an event.

The Box Office Test

The opening numbers will decide how the comeback story is told. Ebenezer earned $1.5 million in Thursday previews. Pre-release tracking had projected an opening weekend of $20 million to $25 million from more than 3,500 North American locations, with around $40 million worldwide — and early reporting after the previews put the three-day opening closer to $17 million to $22 million. That would make it a modest opening, and one that leaves the film depending on word of mouth through the holiday season.

Whether the warm audience reaction or the divided critical one wins out is the question the next few weekends will answer.
