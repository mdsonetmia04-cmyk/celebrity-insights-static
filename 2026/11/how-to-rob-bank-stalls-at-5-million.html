How to Rob a Bank Stalls at $5 Million — David Leitch's Heist Comedy Misfires

Hollywood
Nov 17, 2026
2 min read

How to Rob a Bank opened to $5.1M on a $40–60M budget. The numbers behind David Leitch's heist comedy misfire.

Advertisement
Advertisement
Advertisement

David Leitch's How to Rob a Bank opened to just $5.1 million in North America over the November 13–15 weekend, landing seventh at the box office — a weak start for a film reported to cost between $40 million and $60 million to produce.

Advertisement

The Amazon MGM heist comedy stars Nicholas Hoult, Zoë Kravitz, Anna Sawai, Pete Davidson, Christian Slater and John C. Reilly as a crew of social-media-savvy bank robbers who broadcast their heists online, only to find their viral fame has caught the attention of the federal authorities. Another $5.1 million from international markets brought its worldwide opening to $10.3 million.

Advertisement

The Numbers Behind the Miss

Trade estimates put the film's break-even point near $100 million — roughly two and a half times its reported $40 million budget, before marketing costs. At its current pace, analysts expect it to fall well short of recouping its investment.

Advertisement

Audiences were lukewarm too: the film earned a B- CinemaScore and a 34% critics' score on Rotten Tomatoes, against a 78% audience score. It is Amazon MGM's lowest opening of the year for a film in more than 2,000 theatres, and among the weakest wide-release openings of Hoult's career.

Advertisement

A Director Looking for a Win

Leitch, the director behind Deadpool 2, Hobbs & Shaw and Bullet Train, is coming off The Fall Guy, which also underperformed. How to Rob a Bank was moved from its planned September 4 release to November 13 after its first trailer drew a strong response — a move that has not paid off. With The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping opening next weekend, the film's core young audience is likely to move on quickly.
