Fela Kuti Enters the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame — The First African Solo Artist Ever Inducted

Music
Nov 16, 2026
2 min read

Fela Kuti entered the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame, the first African solo artist inducted. The Afrobeat pioneer's Early Influence honour.

Nearly thirty years after his death, Fela Kuti has taken his place in the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame. The Nigerian musician, bandleader and creator of Afrobeat was inducted on Saturday, November 14, 2026, at the Peacock Theater in Los Angeles, receiving the Early Influence Award — and becoming the first African-born solo artist ever to enter the Hall since its founding in 1986. His family accepted the honour on his behalf, with his son Femi Kuti sharing a family photograph from the ceremony alongside his son Mádé, sister Yeni and brother Seun.

The Man Who Invented Afrobeat

Fela Anikulapo Kuti (1938–1997) was a saxophonist, bandleader, composer and activist whose career ran from the late 1950s to the 1990s. In Lagos in the late 1960s and 1970s he created Afrobeat, fusing highlife, jazz and funk with Yoruba rhythms into a sound that has shaped decades of global music since. The Associated Press's look at the 2026 class named "Water No Get Enemy," "Zombie" and "Let's Start" among his defining songs.

Advertisement

The Early Influence Award recognises artists whose music and performance style significantly shaped rock and roll and influenced popular culture. Artists become eligible for the Hall 25 years after their first release, and Fela's induction had been announced in April 2026. It caps a year of major honours: he also received the Recording Academy's Lifetime Achievement Award in 2026.

A Class of 18

The 2026 ceremony inducted 18 honourees in all. The performer class included Phil Collins, Billy Idol, Iron Maiden, Joy Division and New Order, Oasis, Sade, Luther Vandross and Wu-Tang Clan. Sade Adu, who is Nigerian-born, was inducted in the Performer category — meaning this year's ceremony marked the first time African-born artists have been inducted into the Hall at all. Celia Cruz, Queen Latifah, MC Lyte and Gram Parsons joined Fela in the Early Influence category, with Cruz becoming the first artist inducted who sang primarily in Spanish. Producers Rick Rubin, Arif Mardin and Jimmy Miller and songwriter Linda Creed received the Musical Excellence Award, and variety show host Ed Sullivan was also honoured.

How to Watch the Ceremony

The ceremony was recorded for broadcast and will air on ABC and stream on Disney+ in December. The induction ceremony returns to Cleveland in 2027. For Afrobeat — a sound born in Lagos more than half a century ago — the Los Angeles stage was a long time coming, and the Hall's first African solo inductee is the artist who started it all.
