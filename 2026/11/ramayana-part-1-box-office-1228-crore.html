Ramayana Part 1 Box Office: ₹1,228 Crore Worldwide in 10 Days, Trade Estimates Say

Bollywood
Nov 15, 2026
4 min read

Ramayana Part 1 crossed ₹1,228 crore worldwide in 10 days. Trade estimates, records, and the Ranbir Kapoor epic's box office run.

Ramayana: Part 1 has crossed ₹1,228 crore at the worldwide box office in its first 10 days, according to trade estimates from Sacnilk, the industry tracker most Indian outlets cite for daily collections. The Nitesh Tiwari-directed epic, starring Ranbir Kapoor as Rama, Sai Pallavi as Sita, and Yash as Ravana, opened in theaters worldwide on November 5, 2026, and has held its momentum through its second weekend.

All figures in this post are trade estimates, not official studio numbers. Sacnilk itself notes that its overseas figures are rough estimates subject to change. Final audited totals, if the producers release them, may differ.

The 10-Day Run

The Ramayana box office collection started big and stayed strong. The film opened with ₹64.85 crore net in India on day one, November 5, and finished its first eight days — its opening week, counting from a Thursday release — with about ₹564 crore net in India, per Sacnilk figures cited across trade coverage.

The milestones came quickly after that. The film entered the ₹1,000 crore worldwide club on its eighth day. On day nine, Friday, November 13, it collected ₹48.10 crore net in India, taking its India net total past ₹612 crore and its worldwide gross past ₹1,100 crore — ₹1,110.70 crore, including ₹378.50 crore from overseas markets.

Day 10 brought a second-weekend jump. On Saturday, November 14, the film collected ₹72.90 crore net in India across 23,451 shows, a 51.6% rise over Friday. That took its 10-day India net total to ₹685.60 crore, with an India gross of ₹819.50 crore. Overseas, another estimated ₹30 crore on Saturday brought the international total to ₹408.50 crore, pushing the Ramayana Part 1 worldwide collection to ₹1,228 crore.

The Hindi version continues to drive the business, contributing ₹53.75 crore of day 10's India net total. Kannada followed with ₹5.40 crore, with Tamil, Malayalam, and Telugu versions making up the rest. Overall occupancy on day 10 was 45.5% across tracked shows.

Records and Comparisons

By the end of day 10, the film had passed the lifetime worldwide totals of two of the biggest Hindi blockbusters of recent years — Pathaan (₹1,055 crore) and Jawan (₹1,160 crore) — according to the same trade estimates. It also moved past the lifetime India net collection of Vicky Kaushal's Chhaava during its second week. Trade coverage has described it as the 11th Indian film to cross ₹1,000 crore worldwide, a group that includes Dangal, Baahubali 2, RRR, KGF Chapter 2, and Pushpa 2.

Whether it goes further depends on the coming days. The second Sunday's numbers will show how much of the weekend surge holds, and trade watchers are already looking at whether the film can challenge the all-time worldwide totals of the biggest Indian releases. With no major new release crowding it out this weekend, the run has room to breathe.

The Film Behind the Numbers

Ramayana: Part 1 is the first installment of a planned two-part adaptation of the Ramayana, produced by Namit Malhotra's Prime Focus Studios together with Yash's Monster Mind Creations. Alongside Kapoor, Pallavi, and Yash, the cast includes Ravie Dubey as Lakshman. The film released in multiple languages — Hindi, Kannada, Tamil, Telugu, and Malayalam — which is reflected in the language-wise split of its daily collections.

The scale of the opening is notable even by the standards of recent Indian blockbusters: a ₹1,000 crore worldwide total in eight days, and a second Saturday that grew more than 50% over the second Friday. But the honest caveat stands. These are daily trade estimates compiled from theater-level tracking, and they are revised as final figures come in — day 9's India net total, for example, was reported as both ₹47.85 crore and ₹48.10 crore in different updates before Sacnilk's final day-10 summary settled on the higher figure. Treat the headline totals as the best available estimates, not as final audited grosses.
