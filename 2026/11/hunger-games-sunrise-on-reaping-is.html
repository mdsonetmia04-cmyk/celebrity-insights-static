Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping Is Tracking Toward a $100 Million Opening

Hollywood
Nov 17, 2026
3 min read

Sunrise on the Reaping is tracking toward a $100M opening. Advance sales, forecasts, and the Hunger Games box office story.

Advertisement
Advertisement
Advertisement

Three days before it opens, The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping has gone from a solid prequel bet to the story of the autumn box office. What started as a $55–65 million long-range forecast is now a $100 million conversation — and advance ticket sales are running ahead of Wicked.

Advertisement

The film opens in US cinemas and IMAX on Friday, November 20, 2026, with early-access screenings on Wednesday, November 18 at 6 p.m. It is the sixth film in the franchise and the second prequel, telling the story of the 50th Hunger Games — the Second Quarter Quell — and the young Haymitch Abernathy who won them.

The Tracking Jumped — Here Is the New Range

Box Office Pro's long-range forecast had the film at $55–65 million as recently as late October. Updated tracking reported this week puts the opening weekend at $80–100 million in North America, and Box Office Theory goes further: its current forecast is $105 million-plus, with a $116 million pinpoint and a $140 million high end.

Advertisement

Deadline raised its floor to $80 million or more, reporting that presales are the strongest Lionsgate has logged for any release since the pandemic, running ahead of both Wicked and the studio's own Michael, which opened to $97.2 million. Tracking is a forecast, not a result, and Box Office Theory itself flags the film as "volatile to forecast" — but the direction of travel is unmistakable.

What the First Reactions Said

Full reviews are still embargoed, so there is no Rotten Tomatoes score yet. The only critical word so far comes from short first reactions posted after the October 28 London premiere — and the words that kept coming up were "darker" and "heavier," with several critics calling it the strongest Hunger Games film yet.

Advertisement

Joseph Zada, the newcomer playing 16-year-old Haymitch, drew the most praise. Then, on November 13, Suzanne Collins herself weighed in. In a "letter to the fans" published through Lionsgate, the author called the film "a vibrant and devastating adaptation" and praised Zada for what she called "a remarkably powerful and moving performance."

Advertisement

Why This Opening Matters

The reported budget of $170–200 million means the film has to outperform the last prequel by a wide margin, and the presales race it is winning is against Wicked: For Good, which opens the very next day. If the $100 million-plus forecasts hold, Sunrise on the Reaping will be the biggest Hunger Games opening since the original trilogy — and a reminder that, a decade after Katniss, the franchise still moves tickets.

The verdict arrives on November 20. Until then, the tracking is the story — and it keeps moving in one direction.
