James Gunn and Peter Safran Are Out at DC Studios — What Happens to Superman Now?

Hollywood
Nov 16, 2026
4 min read

James Gunn and Peter Safran are out at DC Studios. What the shake-up means for Superman, the DCU, and Jeremy Latcham's takeover.

Advertisement
Advertisement
Advertisement

The biggest executive shake-up in superhero movies in years is now official. Skydance Motion Picture Group announced on November 9 that James Gunn and Peter Safran are stepping down as co-chairmen and co-CEOs of DC Studios after four years in charge, with Marvel veteran Jeremy Latcham taking over as President and Chief Creative Officer on December 1, 2026. The news landed barely a month after Warner Bros. and Paramount completed their merger into the new Skydance, and it immediately raised the same question across Hollywood: what happens to the DC Universe Gunn and Safran built?

Advertisement

Why They Left

Gunn and Safran were appointed by Warner Bros. Discovery in late 2022 to bring DC's film and television output under one creative direction. Their rebooted DC Universe launched with the animated series Creature Commandos and continued with Superman, Peacemaker, Supergirl, Lanterns and Clayface. Gunn directed and co-wrote Superman himself; the film grossed more than $618 million worldwide.

Advertisement

In a farewell note after the announcement, Gunn described his time as studio chief as "trying, to say the least," and said he was "incredibly relieved and excited to get back to pure filmmaking." He said the demands of running a studio did not sit well alongside directing, and that returning to writing and directing was what drew him to the job in the first place. In their joint statement, Gunn and Safran said they were confident in Latcham and looked forward to returning to storytelling.

Advertisement

Latcham is a longtime Marvel Studios executive who produced foundational Marvel Cinematic Universe films including Iron Man and The Avengers. He will report to Skydance Motion Picture Group co-chairs Dana Goldberg and Josh Greenstein, overseeing DC's film, television and animation slate.

Superman Is Safe — For Now

The most urgent question for fans was the fate of Man of Tomorrow, the Superman sequel Gunn is directing. The answer, so far, is reassuring: the film is in post-production, Gunn remains its writer-director, Safran is producing, and its release date of July 9, 2027 is unchanged. David Corenswet, Rachel Brosnahan and Nicholas Hoult return as Superman, Lois Lane and Lex Luthor. Gunn has called making the film the best filmmaking experience of his life.

Advertisement

On November 14, Gunn also took to social media to deny a rumor that Skydance executives had seen an early cut and ordered sweeping changes. "No one from Skydance has even seen the movie," he wrote, adding that only Safran and a small trusted group of friends and family had. He has previously said the film is nowhere near ready for a trailer in 2026.

Advertisement

Gunn and Safran also remain producers on Matt Reeves' The Batman Part II, starring Robert Pattinson, which is in production, and on the 2027 miniseries The People v. Gorilla Grodd. Gunn has said he is writing one more DC film, with characters he and Safran set up to outlive their tenure, though he has not disclosed its title.

Advertisement

What Comes Next

The handover takes effect on December 1, and Latcham inherits a slate in motion rather than a blank page. The commercial record he inherits is mixed: Superman was a $618 million success, while Supergirl stumbled at the box office, according to trade reporting. Whether Latcham keeps the ten-year plan Gunn and Safran laid out, or charts a new one, will become clear only after he takes the chair. For now, the next chapter of Clark Kent and Lex Luthor's story is still in the hands of the people who started it.
